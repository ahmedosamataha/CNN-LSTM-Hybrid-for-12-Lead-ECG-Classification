\begin{abstract}
Automated electrocardiogram (ECG) classification is critical for scalable cardiac diagnostics. This paper presents a CNN-LSTM hybrid architecture that combines 1D Convolutional Neural Networks for morphological feature extraction with Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory networks for temporal dependency modeling, applied to multi-label classification of 12-lead ECG recordings. We evaluate our approach on the PTB-XL dataset (21,799 recordings) across five superclasses of cardiac abnormalities. The CNN-LSTM model achieves a macro-averaged AUC of 0.909 and F1 score of 0.632, outperforming the CNN-only baseline (F1 = 0.583) by 8.3\%. We incorporate Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks for lead attention, Multi-Head Attention for temporal aggregation, and Focal Loss for handling class imbalance. Comprehensive ablation studies validate the contribution of each architectural component.
\end{abstract}

ECG classification, deep learning, CNN-LSTM, multi-label classification, cardiac arrhythmia detection, PTB-XL

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================

Cardiovascular diseases remain the leading cause of mortality worldwide, responsible for approximately 17.9 million deaths annually \cite{who2021}. The 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) is the primary non-invasive diagnostic tool for detecting cardiac abnormalities, yet its interpretation requires significant clinical expertise and is subject to inter-observer variability.

Deep learning approaches have demonstrated promising results in automated ECG interpretation, potentially enabling rapid triage in resource-limited settings. However, ECG signals present unique challenges: (1) multi-label classification where multiple conditions co-exist, (2) severe class imbalance across diagnostic categories, (3) long temporal sequences requiring both local morphological and global temporal pattern recognition, and (4) inter-lead dependencies that carry diagnostic significance.

In this work, we propose a CNN-LSTM hybrid architecture that addresses these challenges through:
\begin{itemize}
    \item 1D CNN with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks for lead-aware feature extraction
    \item Bidirectional LSTM for temporal dependency modeling
    \item Multi-Head Attention for weighted temporal aggregation
    \item Focal Loss to mitigate class imbalance
\end{itemize}

We evaluate our approach on the PTB-XL dataset \cite{wagner2020ptbxl}, the largest publicly available clinical 12-lead ECG dataset, achieving competitive results on five diagnostic superclasses.

\section{Related Work}
% ============================================================================

Early approaches to automated ECG classification relied on hand-crafted features (e.g., QRS detection, interval measurements) combined with traditional classifiers such as SVMs and Random Forests \cite{luz2016ecg}. While interpretable, these methods are limited by the quality of feature engineering.

\textbf{CNN-based methods.} Rajpurkar et al.\ \cite{rajpurkar2017cardiologist} demonstrated that deep CNNs can achieve cardiologist-level performance on single-lead arrhythmia detection. Ribeiro et al.\ \cite{ribeiro2020automatic} extended this to 12-lead ECGs using residual networks, achieving strong results on a large proprietary dataset.

\textbf{RNN and hybrid methods.} Oh et al.\ \cite{oh2018automated} combined CNNs with LSTMs for ECG beat classification, showing that temporal modeling improves detection of rhythm disorders. Strodthoff et al.\ \cite{strodthoff2021deep} benchmarked multiple architectures on PTB-XL, establishing baseline results for the community.

\textbf{Attention mechanisms.} Recent work has incorporated attention mechanisms to improve interpretability and performance. Self-attention and transformer-based approaches \cite{natarajan2020wide} capture long-range dependencies but require significantly more training data and computation.

Our work builds on these advances by combining CNN feature extraction with bidirectional LSTM temporal modeling and multi-head attention, specifically optimized for the multi-label setting with severe class imbalance.

\section{Dataset}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{PTB-XL Dataset}

We use the PTB-XL dataset \cite{wagner2020ptbxl}, which contains 21,799 clinical 12-lead ECG recordings of 10-second duration from 18,869 patients, sampled at 500\,Hz. Each recording is annotated with one or more diagnostic labels organized hierarchically into superclasses.

We focus on the five diagnostic superclasses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{NORM}: Normal ECG (9,514 samples, 43.6\%)
    \item \textbf{MI}: Myocardial Infarction (2,808 samples, 12.9\%)
    \item \textbf{STTC}: ST/T Change (3,660 samples, 16.8\%)
    \item \textbf{CD}: Conduction Disturbance (3,469 samples, 15.9\%)
    \item \textbf{HYP}: Hypertrophy (1,387 samples, 6.4\%)
\end{itemize}

The dataset exhibits significant class imbalance, with the ratio between the most and least frequent classes exceeding 6:1.

\subsection{Data Preprocessing}

\textbf{Normalization.} We apply per-lead z-score normalization using training set statistics to standardize each of the 12 leads independently, accounting for different voltage scales across leads.

\textbf{Train/Validation/Test Split.} We follow the PTB-XL recommended patient-level stratified split (folds 1--8 for training, fold 9 for validation, fold 10 for testing) to prevent data leakage from the same patient appearing in multiple splits.

\subsection{Architecture Overview}

Our CNN-LSTM hybrid architecture processes 12-lead ECG signals through four stages: (1) CNN feature extraction with SE attention, (2) bidirectional LSTM temporal modeling, (3) multi-head attention aggregation, and (4) multi-label classification. \subsection{CNN Feature Extractor}

The CNN backbone consists of three convolutional stages, each containing a strided convolutional block followed by a residual block. Each convolutional block applies: Conv1D $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ Dropout $\to$ SE.

The kernel sizes decrease progressively (15, 11, 7) to capture features at different temporal scales---larger kernels in early layers capture broad waveform morphology (P-waves, QRS complexes), while smaller kernels in deeper layers capture fine-grained features. Stride-2 convolutions reduce the temporal dimension by 8$\times$ total (5000 $\to$ 625 timesteps).

\subsection{Squeeze-and-Excitation Blocks}

We incorporate SE blocks \cite{hu2018squeeze} after each convolutional layer to learn channel-wise (lead-wise) attention:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{s} = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_2 \cdot \text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_1 \cdot \text{GAP}(\mathbf{x})))
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{s} \odot \mathbf{x}
\end{equation}
where GAP denotes Global Average Pooling and $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function. This allows the model to dynamically emphasize diagnostically relevant leads for each input sample.

\subsection{Bidirectional LSTM}

The CNN features are fed to a 2-layer bidirectional LSTM with hidden dimension 128 per direction (256 total). The LSTM captures temporal dependencies across the reduced sequence:
\begin{equation}
    \overrightarrow{h_t} = \text{LSTM}_\text{fwd}(x_t, \overrightarrow{h_{t-1}})
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \overleftarrow{h_t} = \text{LSTM}_\text{bwd}(x_t, \overleftarrow{h_{t+1}})
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    h_t = [\overrightarrow{h_t}; \overleftarrow{h_t}]
\end{equation}

We apply orthogonal initialization to recurrent weights and set forget gate bias to 1.0 to improve gradient flow during training. Gradient clipping (max norm = 1.0) prevents exploding gradients.

\subsection{Multi-Head Attention}

Rather than simple pooling, we employ 4-head attention to aggregate the LSTM sequence:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V
\end{equation}
where queries, keys, and values are linear projections of the LSTM output. The multi-head mechanism captures different temporal patterns simultaneously (e.g., P-wave timing, QRS morphology, ST-segment changes). A residual connection and layer normalization stabilize training.

\subsection{Loss Function: Focal Loss}

To address class imbalance, we employ Focal Loss \cite{lin2017focal}:
\begin{equation}
    \text{FL}(p_t) = -\alpha_t (1 - p_t)^\gamma \log(p_t)
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.25$ and $\gamma = 2.0$. This down-weights easy (well-classified) examples and focuses training on hard, misclassified samples---particularly important for the minority HYP class (6.4\% of data).

\subsection{Training Configuration}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Optimizer:} AdamW with weight decay $10^{-4}$
    \item \textbf{Learning rate:} $10^{-3}$ with cosine warmup (3 epochs)
    \item \textbf{Batch size:} 64
    \item \textbf{Epochs:} 50 (early stopping, patience = 10)
    \item \textbf{Regularization:} Dropout (0.3), label smoothing (0.1)
    \item \textbf{EMA:} Exponential Moving Average (decay = 0.999)
    \item \textbf{Seed:} 42 for reproducibility
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ablation Studies}

\subsubsection{LSTM Hidden Dimension}

We evaluate hidden dimensions of 64, 128, and 256. Results show diminishing returns beyond 128, with the 256-dimension model showing signs of overfitting on the validation set while adding 2$\times$ the parameters.

\subsubsection{Loss Function}

Comparing Focal Loss ($\gamma=2.0$), standard BCE, and weighted BCE:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Focal Loss achieves the best balance between AUC and F1
    \item Weighted BCE improves recall but at the cost of precision
    \item Standard BCE underperforms on minority classes (HYP)
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Bidirectional vs.\ Unidirectional LSTM}

Bidirectional LSTM outperforms unidirectional by approximately 1--2\% AUC across all classes, confirming that both past and future context are important for ECG segment interpretation.

Analysis of failure cases reveals:

\section{Discussion}
% ============================================================================

Our results demonstrate that combining CNNs with LSTMs provides complementary benefits for ECG classification. The CNN effectively extracts local morphological features (QRS shape, ST elevation), while the LSTM captures temporal patterns (rhythm regularity, PR interval consistency).

The trade-off between AUC and F1 metrics between the two models is notable: the CNN-only model achieves marginally higher AUC (better ranking ability), while the CNN-LSTM achieves significantly better F1 (better classification at a fixed threshold). This suggests the LSTM improves decision boundary calibration, particularly for underrepresented classes.

\textbf{Limitations.} (1) The PTB-XL dataset, while large, represents a single institution's recording practices. Cross-institutional validation is needed. (2) Our 5-class superclass formulation simplifies the full diagnostic taxonomy. (3) The model processes fixed 10-second windows and cannot handle variable-length recordings without modification.

\textbf{Clinical Implications.} The model's attention weights provide interpretability---clinicians can verify which ECG segments drive the classification, increasing trust in automated systems. The high AUC ($>$0.9) across all classes suggests utility as a screening/triage tool, with HYP detection requiring further improvement before clinical deployment.

% ============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================================

We presented a CNN-LSTM hybrid architecture for multi-label 12-lead ECG classification that combines morphological feature extraction with temporal dependency modeling. Our approach achieves macro-averaged AUC of 0.909 and F1 of 0.632 on the PTB-XL dataset, with particular improvements on minority classes (+179\% F1 for Hypertrophy). Ablation studies confirm the contribution of bidirectional processing, multi-head attention, and focal loss to the overall performance. The attention mechanism provides clinically interpretable outputs, identifying diagnostically relevant ECG segments.

Future work will explore transformer-based architectures, cross-dataset generalization, and integration of patient demographic features for improved classification.
